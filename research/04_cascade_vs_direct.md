Two architectures, identical CV folds + identical temporal split,
same model factories (logreg, rforest, hgb) on both branches.

- **Direct**: one 4-class classifier predicting (None, Kraken,
  Triton, Coral) in a single fit.
- **Cascade**: tier-1 binary predicts P(drug) → tier-2 multiclass
  predicts P(Kraken | drug) / P(Triton | drug) / P(Coral | drug)
  on the drug-positive cohort. Combined:
  `P(None) = 1 − P(drug)`,
  `P(K) = P(drug) · P(K | drug)` etc. All probabilities sum to 1.

## Why the cascade helps on the holdout

1. **Decomposition aligns with where signal is**. Tier-1 (drug vs no-drug)
   is a 60/40 problem driven by the strong sympathomimetic-Kraken
   triage signature plus the festival flag. Tier-2 (which drug) is a
   37/32/31 problem on a smaller cohort with relatively more
   balanced classes. Both are individually easier than the direct
   40/22/20/18 4-class problem.
2. **Tier-2 trains on a cleaner cohort**. None-class patients have
   typical medical pathology — their triage signature is wildly
   heterogeneous. Excluding them from tier-2 training prevents that
   noise from being absorbed into the drug-class decision boundary.
3. **Calibration improves**. Cascade log-loss is lower than direct
   on every (model × split) combination (max log-loss gain: rforest
   holdout 1.218 → 1.209, a +1% reduction). Probability outputs are
   more useful for downstream thresholding (e.g. the Task-3 triage
   calculator's evidence trail).

## Why the cascade doesn't win on CV macro AUC

- CV folds randomly stratify on the 4-class outcome, so each fold
  has 4-5 patients per drug class. The variance of macro AUC across
  such small held-out folds is high (rforest direct CV AUC
  std = 0.053). The 0.004 macro-AUC loss on CV is well within fold
  noise.
- The cascade also adds compounding error: tier-1's mistake on
  P(drug) is amplified through tier-2's P(K | drug). On CV, this
  compounding shows up as slightly noisier macro AUC.
- The holdout doesn't have this issue because both architectures
  are evaluated on the same 74-patient test set with single-pass
  predictions.

## Recommendation

**Ship the cascade for deployment.** On the production-relevant
temporal-holdout metric, rforest cascade gives:

- macro AUC 0.712 vs direct 0.697 (+0.015)
- macro PR-AUC 0.449 vs direct 0.408 (+0.041)
- accuracy 0.460 vs direct 0.392 (+0.068)
- AUC Kraken 0.656 vs direct 0.621 (+0.035)
- AUC None  0.724 vs direct 0.714 (+0.010)

The cascade also produces tier-1 and tier-2 probabilities
separately, which is what the Task-3 triage calculator already
wants (the calculator displays "drug-positive likelihood" plus
"most likely drug" as two distinct cards).
