## Why does prevalence-guessing beat a real model on T vs C?

Section §7g of RUNBOOK documents the empirical finding: a
Triton-vs-Coral classifier trained on triage data lands at or
**below** chance on the holdout (ROC-AUC 0.32-0.39 across models;
all BSS values negative). The data simply doesn't contain
triage-time signal for this discrimination — Triton's and Coral's
defining tokens live in HPI/MDM.

So Cascade C, by fitting a real model to noise, produces actively
**worse** P(T) and P(C) estimates than Cascade B, which assigns the
non-Kraken probability mass to T and C in proportion to the
training-set prevalence. The prevalence baseline is uninformative
but **calibrated** (no false confidence in either direction).
A sub-chance model is **anti-calibrated** — its high-confidence
predictions are systematically wrong.

This is the textbook "don't fit models to noise" lesson made
concrete with 0.026 macro-AUC headroom on the holdout (B = 0.725
vs C = 0.699 for hgb).

## Per-class behaviour explains the rest

- **None AUC**: Cascade B inherits its P(None) from the tier-1 binary
  alone — a stronger model (0.717 holdout ROC-AUC per §7c) than
  the 4-class direct model's implicit one-vs-rest None probability.
- **Kraken AUC**: Cascade B's P(K) = P(drug) × P(K | drug). Both
  stages are high-quality models (§7c tier-1 0.717 ROC-AUC, §7f
  Kraken-vs-rest 0.825). The product is well-calibrated.
- **Triton / Coral AUC**: Cascade B's P(T) and P(C) are smoothed
  toward each other (separated only by prevalence), which lifts
  per-class AUC modestly. Cascade C's model-driven split adds
  variance without signal.

## Deployment recommendation

**Ship Cascade B with hgb** (or rforest as a close second).

```
P(None)   = 1 - P(drug)
P(K)      = P(drug) · P(K | drug)
P(T)      = P(drug) · (1 - P(K | drug)) · π_T
P(C)      = P(drug) · (1 - P(K | drug)) · (1 - π_T)
```

where π_T is the training-set prevalence of Triton among non-Kraken
drug-positive patients (currently ≈ 0.515).

This requires only two trained models (tier-1 binary §7c + Kraken-
vs-rest §7f), plus one scalar prevalence constant. Smaller surface
area, better calibration, better holdout AUC.

The previous recommendation (Cascade-A from
[`research/04_cascade_vs_direct.md`](04_cascade_vs_direct.md)) still
beats direct 4-class but is now superseded by Cascade-B.
